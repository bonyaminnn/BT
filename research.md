# Gridmarc — Phase 1 research

## Method and limits

- **Reference site.** weaveos.com is an engineering-analytics product. It uses a calm, minimal layout with plenty of white space and a recurring illustrated mascot. We take only its structure and mood: wide illustrated scenes at the top and bottom, quiet text sections in between, and small spot illustrations as pauses. We use none of its copy, artwork or brand.
- **Competitor sources.** The sandbox could not load the agency sites directly, so this analysis uses search results: the agencies' own service pages where they were indexed, plus Clutch, DesignRush and Sortlist profiles. Figures marked "self-reported" come from the agencies themselves and were not verified.

### Patterns worth noting

1. **Big agencies sell on scale**: headcount, logos, awards and project counts. Gridmarc cannot win that game and shouldn't try.
2. **Everyone says "end to end"**, but in a large agency it usually means a hand-off chain: strategist, designer, developer, PM. The phrase has lost meaning, and that leaves room for whoever can make it concrete.
3. **Designjoy shows that "tiny" can be the premium angle.** It turns small size into speed, directness and a simple offer, and it publishes clear terms.
4. **Senior attention is the hidden selling point.** Clay leads with "co-founders lead the team" and Phenomenon with "one senior team owns it". Buyers are afraid of being handed to juniors.
5. **Premium homepages are sparse.** They use one strong claim, real work, a clear process and one call to action. Long feature lists read as cheap.

## My audience

**Who:** Founders, product owners and marketing or ops leads at small and mid-sized businesses. They fall into two groups:
- **Have something:** a live website or app that is slow, dated, hard to update or not converting, and they need a team that can work inside it.
- **Starting fresh:** a new product, app or site, where they need one team to take it from idea to launch.

**What they want**
- One accountable team, not five vendors and a project manager in between.
- Speed: weeks, not quarters, and quick replies on a normal Tuesday.
- Design and engineering that fit together, so what was designed is what ships.
- Someone who can fix and improve the current product without throwing it away.
- To know what it costs and what they get before they commit.

**What they doubt**
- "Two people? What happens if one gets sick, or the project grows?"
- "Is this senior work or a freelancer collective?"
- "Will they disappear after launch?"
- "Can they really do both design and development well?"
- "Will time zones or communication slow us down?"

**What makes them buy**
- Seeing the actual people who will do the work, and knowing those people won't change.
- Glimpses of real, recent work that looks like what they need.
- A clear, short process with named steps and a defined first week.
- Fixed scopes or simple plans, so they're never surprised.
- A fast, human first reply. The contact step itself shows the speed we promise.

## My positioning

> **Gridmarc is a two-person design and development studio. The people you talk to are the people who design, build and support your product, so you get end-to-end work faster, with nothing lost in hand-offs.**

Short form for the site: *Two people. Your whole product. No hand-offs.*

## 3 key messages the homepage has to land

1. **You work with the makers.** No account managers, no juniors, no hand-off chain. The two people on the first call design it, build it and answer your messages.
2. **End to end, and quick.** Strategy, design, development and ongoing support from one small team. Fewer meetings and less translation mean you ship in weeks.
3. **We meet you where you are.** We can improve the product you already have or build a new one from a blank page, and we stay on after launch.

## The illustration world: "The Survey"

**Theme.** One quiet, open valley, drawn as if a surveyor's grid were faintly laid over the land. Two small figures, the two of us, mark it, plan it and build on it. Over the full set of illustrations the valley changes from an empty field with chalk grid lines to a small, finished settlement with lit windows.

**Why it fits Gridmarc**
- **The name.** A *grid* is the plan and a *marc* (mark) is the survey stake that fixes a point. Surveyors set the marks and builders follow them. That is what we do for digital products.
- **The two client types.** Some clients bring land that is already built on (an existing product), where we survey, repair and extend. Others bring an empty plot (a new build), where we lay the grid and raise the first walls.
- **Two people.** The same two figures appear in every scene: one with a drafting board (design), one with tools (build). They always work together and are never in a crowd. The world shows our size as a strength: small, deliberate and hands-on.
- **Continuity.** The hero is the morning survey. The footer is the same valley at dusk, built and lit. The site tells the story from first mark to finished product.

**One consistent style.** Soft hand-painted watercolour washes over fine, slightly loose ink linework on warm off-white paper. A faint pencil grid shows through in the larger scenes, and the palette is restrained. Figures are small, faceless and gestural. Nothing is photographic or 3D, there are no gradients, and the landscape is generic and placeless (rolling hills, a stream, low trees), with no recognisable country or landmark. Full rules are in `assets.md`.
